## Explaining it (for the video)

**One sentence:** An AI that notices when what you *say* you want doesn't match what you *do*, and uses a bigger model only when it's unsure about you.

**30-second version:** Most assistants save "I like short answers" and treat it as fact. But people say one thing and keep asking for another, like "keep it short" and then "elaborate" three times. We keep two profiles, what you *said* and what you *do*. When they clash, the agent is unsure about you, so it escalates to a stronger Nemotron model. When they agree, a small cheap model is enough. Every choice is logged with a plain reason.

**Key terms (say these):**
- **Stated profile**: what you told it you prefer.
- **Observed profile**: what it learned from how you actually ask.
- **Dissonance**: the gap between the two, scored 0 to 1.
- **Routing by uncertainty, not difficulty**: most routers ask "is the question hard?" We ask "how sure are we about this person?"
- **Nano / Super / Ultra**: the three Nemotron sizes. Score under 0.4 goes to Nano, 0.4 to 0.7 to Super, 0.7 and up to Ultra.
- **Reconciling**: on Ultra, one answer serves both sides: a one-line TL;DR for the "short" you said, then the depth you keep asking for.
- **Decision log**: every answer records which model and why, in words a person can read.

**The six guidelines in one line each:** words are claims, behavior is evidence · one-off requests teach nothing and old habits fade · this message's instruction wins · spend power only where it matters · explain every choice, never change a preference silently · be honest and private.

**The honesty angle (our differentiator):** we show three cases, not one. Success cases, failure cases with a reason code (missed signal, false signal, over-routed), and false-claim checks (every explanation is verified against the database; invented links are flagged). "Failures are where the efficiency story lives."

**The efficiency number:** same conversations run three ways, always-Nano, always-Ultra and the router. Fill in with real Token Factory numbers; simulated so far: router about 5x cheaper than always-Ultra, with no under-served turns.

**Demo flow (about 2 min):** set "concise" → ask for more detail twice → watch it escalate to Ultra → TL;DR plus depth → it asks "update your preference?" → accept → cost drops back to Nano. Then show the eval report: a failure with its code and a caught false claim.

**Be careful to say honestly:** the detector is keyword-based (English only); the "ideal tier" in the eval is our own annotation; cost figures are estimates until checked against Nebius's price page.
